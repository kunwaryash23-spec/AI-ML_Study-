### Thread 1 — PyTorch, built up from the gradient

**Day 27 — tensors and autograd** (`Day_27/PyTorch.ipynb`)

- Tensor ranks laid out side by side: scalar (0-D), vector `(3,)`, matrix `(2,2)`, `torch.zeros(2,3)`, `torch.rand` under `manual_seed(0)`. Seeding first is the habit that makes every later result reproducible.
- `x = torch.tensor(3.0, requires_grad=True)` → `y = x**2` → `y.backward()` → `x.grad == 6.0`. You watched autograd record the graph and walk it back.
- A single neuron by hand: `z = w*x + b`, squared-error loss, `loss.backward()`, then read `w.grad` and `b.grad` directly. Doing this *before* touching `nn.Module` is the right order — most people never see the gradient they are optimising.
- A 200-step training loop written manually on `Y = 2X + 1`: forward → MSE → `backward()` → `with torch.no_grad(): w -= 0.1 * w.grad` → `w.grad.zero_()`.

  Two things in that loop matter more than they look. **`no_grad()`** stops the weight update itself from being recorded as part of the graph. **`.grad.zero_()`** exists because PyTorch *accumulates* gradients — forget it and step 2 optimises using step 1's gradient plus its own. That is the single most common silent PyTorch bug, and you wrote the fix by hand.

**Day 28 — the same loop, handed to the framework** (`Day_28/Py_torch.ipynb`)

- Rebuilt Day 27's linear fit as `nn.Linear(1,1)` + `nn.MSELoss()` + `torch.optim.SGD(lr=0.1)`. Same maths, three objects: `optimizer.zero_grad()` replaces your manual `.zero_()`, `optimizer.step()` replaces the manual nudge. Seeing the mapping is the point of doing it twice.
- Then a real classifier: `load_digits`, stratified 75/25 split, `StandardScaler` **fitted on train only** and applied to test. You have kept that discipline consistently since the Day 21 leak note — good.

## Worth fixing

1. **`Day_28/Py_torch.ipynb` — you computed test accuracy and never looked at it.**
   ```python
   with torch.no_grad():
       acc = (model(Xte).argmax(1) == yte).float().mean()   # ← assigned, never printed
   ```
   Training loss of 0.045 tells you the model memorised 1347 examples. It says nothing about the 450 it hasn't seen. Add `print(acc.item())` — and compare it to Day 22's logistic-regression baseline of **0.978**, which is the number your MLP has to beat to have earned its complexity.

2. **`loss_tensor.append(loss)` stores live graph nodes.** Appending the tensor keeps its whole computation graph alive for all 100 epochs — that is a memory leak, and it is why you then needed `.detach().numpy()` to plot. Append `loss.item()` instead and the plotting cell disappears.

3. **`lr=0.5` on full-batch SGD is aggressive.** It worked here, but it is the first thing to suspect when a loss curve goes flat or NaN. Worth one run at `0.1` and one with `Adam` to feel the difference.
