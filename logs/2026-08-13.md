## Worth remembering

**You built RAG's retrieval half before you touched an LLM.** Day 23 (vectorise) → Day 24 (similarity) → Day 25 (chunk + retrieve) → Day 26 (generate). Most people start at the LLM call and treat retrieval as a black box; you did it the other way round, so when a retriever returns garbage you will know where to look. Keep that ordering habit.

**The `Normalizer()` in your Day 25 pipeline is not decoration.** Without it, `index @ q` is a raw dot product, and long chunks would beat relevant chunks simply for having larger magnitude. Normalising first is what makes the one-line scoring correct. If retrieval ever goes strange, check that step first.

**Your project's chunker is better than your lesson's chunker — notice that.** Day 25 slices `text[start:end]` and will happily cut a word in half. `RAG_lyrics` accumulates whole segments up to a size budget, so chunks end on real boundaries and carry timestamp metadata. That is the production pattern. Consider backporting it to the Day 25 pipeline.

**TF-IDF retrieval is keyword matching wearing a costume.** Your lyrics query worked because the words matched exactly. Ask it "how does a parent feel watching a child grow up" and it will likely fail on the same song, because TF-IDF has no idea those words are related. That gap is precisely what real embedding models (`sentence-transformers`, Gemini embeddings) fix — and it is the natural next lesson.

**`Day_23/vector.ipynb` has a broken import cell** — a bare `from` on its own line, plus several duplicated imports. It does not error today only because you re-ran the cells out of order. Clean it up; a notebook that only runs in one specific click-order is a notebook you cannot trust.

**A CV score of 1.000 on 60 reviews is not a result.** `cross_val_score` returned a perfect 1.0 for the CountVectorizer + LogisticRegression pipeline on your hand-written corpus. That is because you wrote the reviews yourself, so positive and negative examples use cleanly disjoint vocabulary. Real reviews contain "the food was good but the service was terrible." Treat 1.000 as a signal the task is too easy, not that the model is excellent.
