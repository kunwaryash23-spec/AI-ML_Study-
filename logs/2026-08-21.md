### `Notebooks/tokenizer.ipynb` — padding, and where the bugs live

This is the messier working copy (17 cells) where you pushed past Day 30 into batching. Good instincts here:

- `special_tokens=["[PAD]", "[UNK]"]` — putting `[PAD]` first so it gets **id 0** is the correct move, and it's why `nn.Embedding(..., padding_idx=0)` will work later.
- You wrote a loop to find the longest sequence (`initial_count`) instead of guessing the max length. Right idea.

But three real bugs, all worth internalising:

1. **`max_len` is hardcoded to 25** in the padding cell while `initial_count` computes the real answer right above it and is then ignored. Use the computed value.
2. **`[0] * (-5)` returns `[]`** — you tested this yourself in cell 15, which was a good reflex. The consequence: if a sentence is *longer* than `max_len`, your padding loop silently does nothing and leaves a ragged row. `torch.tensor()` will then throw a shape error far from the real cause. Padding needs a truncation branch: `ids[:max_len] + [0] * max(0, max_len - len(ids))`.
3. **A missing comma builds a phantom review**:
   ```python
   "what a waste of time"
   "faster than the car"     # ← no comma on the line above
   ```
   Python silently concatenates adjacent string literals, so that's **one** review reading `"what a waste of timefaster than the car"`, not two. The list has 4 items and `labels` has 4 items, so nothing crashes — the data is just quietly wrong. This is the most dangerous class of bug in ML: no error, worse model. When a list of strings is your dataset, `assert len(reviews) == len(labels)` and print `len(reviews)` before training.

Also: cell 7 raised `NameError: name 'raw_ids' is not defined` because it was run before cell 8 defined it. Notebooks let you run out of order; your future self won't remember the order. *Restart & Run All* is the only honest test.

### Day 31 — Chroma, and a retrieval result that should bother you

`Day_31/RAG_Again.ipynb` — 287 restaurant reviews, LSA embeddings, stored and queried in a real vector DB.

- First attempt (cell 3) had the classic mistake and you caught it yourself:
  ```python
  def embed(query: str):
      lsa = make_pipeline(TfidfVectorizer(), TruncatedSVD(n_components=162))
      embeddings = lsa.fit_transform(chunks)   # fits on chunks, ignores query, returns nothing
  ```
  Cell 5 is the fix: **fit once outside the function, `.transform()` inside it.** That fit/transform split is the single most important habit in scikit-learn — the same discipline as the scaler data-leak note from 2026-08-06.
- Chroma wiring is correct: `create_collection(metadata={"hnsw:space": "cosine"})`, `col.add(ids=..., embeddings=..., documents=...)`, `col.query(query_embeddings=..., n_results=2)`. Choosing cosine explicitly rather than taking the L2 default matters, because your LSA vectors aren't normalised.
- **But look at the result you got:**
  ```
  query: "staff and fresh food"
  → ['the coffee was strong and delicious', 'the restaurant offered excellent value']
  ```
  Neither hit mentions staff or freshness. The retrieval is wrong, and the cause is one line: `TruncatedSVD(n_components=2)`. You are compressing 287 documents into a **2-dimensional** space. Two numbers cannot separate "friendly staff" from "good coffee" — everything collapses onto the same handful of points, and the nearest neighbour is essentially arbitrary. Your Day 25 pipeline used 20 components; your own first draft in cell 3 used 162. Bump it back to ~150 and re-run the same query. **This is the most valuable thing in the repo right now**: a working pipeline returning bad answers, with a single knob explaining it.

`Day_32/RAG_Chroma_Syllabus.ipynb` *(this was the untitled notebook sitting in the repo root; renamed and filed — see housekeeping below)*

- PyMuPDF (`fitz`) over `Day_25/Micro_Syallabus.pdf`, `chunk_size=1000` with `overlap=30`, then TF-IDF → Chroma.
- It fails with:
  ```
  ValueError: Unequal lengths for fields: ids: 1, metadatas: 5, embeddings: 1, documents: 1
  ```
  The cause is `chunks = [chunk]` near the top of the Chroma cell. The `for i, chunk in enumerate(chunks)` print-loop in the previous cell leaves `chunk` bound to the **last** chunk, and then that line rebinds `chunks` to a one-item list containing it. Every chunk but the final one is gone; the metadata list still has 5 entries, and Chroma correctly refuses.
  - Fix: delete that line, and don't reuse `chunk`/`chunks` as both loop variable and collection.
  - Also: an `overlap` of 30 on a `chunk_size` of 1000 is 3%. Overlap exists so a sentence split across a boundary still appears whole in one chunk — 10–20% (100–200 chars here) is the usual range.
